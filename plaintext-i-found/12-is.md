# 12 — IS

This example continues from **END**. Its final ciphertext rune lies on a horizontal `I–R–I` mirror. Three connected mirrors on the same row lead to the next key and the two ciphertext runes that decrypt as **IS**.

**Notation:** Coordinates are **1-based** and written `(row, column)`. Rune numbers are indices in the **29-rune alphabet**. `φ` denotes Euler's totient function, and `μ` denotes the Möbius function.

## 1. Find the connected three-rune nodes

The preceding **END** ciphertext finishes at **`I (21,23)`**. This cell is the right endpoint of `I–R–I`. On row 21, four mirror arrangements form a connected chain:

| Mirror | Left endpoint | Center | Right endpoint | Radius |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| `I–R–I` | I (21,21) | R (21,22) | **I (21,23)** | 1 |
| `H–R–H` | **H (21,17)** | R (21,22) | H (21,27) | 5 |
| `H–L–H` | H (21,5) | **L (21,11)** | **H (21,17)** | 6 |
| `EO–L–EO` | **EO (21,9)** | **L (21,11)** | EO (21,13) | 2 |

Each radius counts horizontal **grid cells** from the center to either outer rune. The connections are exact:

1. `I–R–I` and `H–R–H` share **`R (21,22)`**.
2. `H–R–H` and `H–L–H` share **`H (21,17)`**.
3. `H–L–H` and `EO–L–EO` share **`L (21,11)`**.

In this proposed reading, **`H–R–H` provides the key**, while the last two mirrors provide a route to the ciphertext start, **`EO (21,9)`**.

## 2. Derive and align the decryption key

### Replace the center rune

Keep the two outer `H` runes. Apply `φ` to the index of the center `R`:

`R = 4 → φ(4) = 2 → TH`

The unrotated key is **`H–TH–H`**.

### Calculate its rotation

Apply `φ` to each rune index of this derived key, then apply `μ` to the resulting totient values:

| Key rune | Rune index | Totient | Möbius value |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| H | 8 | φ(8) = 4 | μ(4) = 0 |
| TH | 2 | φ(2) = 1 | μ(1) = +1 |
| H | 8 | φ(8) = 4 | μ(4) = 0 |

The **totient signature** is `(4, 1, 4)`, and the **Möbius signature** is `(0, +1, 0)`.

The Möbius sum specifies the number of cyclic left rotations:

`Rotation = (0 + 1 + 0) mod 3 = 1`

Apply **one cyclic left rotation**:

`H–TH–H → TH–H–H`

**Final key: `TH–H–H`.** Since the selected ciphertext has only **two runes**, its decryption uses the first two key positions: **`TH–H`**.

### A matching signature at the previous endpoint

The smaller `I–R–I` mirror also has center `R = 4`. Replacing its center by `φ(4) = 2` produces `I–TH–I`:

`φ(I = 10) = 4;  φ(TH = 2) = 1;  φ(I = 10) = 4`

Thus **`I–TH–I` and `H–TH–H` have the same totient signature `(4, 1, 4)`**, despite having different outer runes. This is a numerical connection between the preceding endpoint mirror and the selected key.

## 3. Locate the ciphertext start

Starting from the shared **`H (21,17)`**, follow the connected mirrors leftward:

`H (21,17) → left 6 → L (21,11) → left 2 → EO (21,9)`

The two distances come directly from the mirror radii:

- **6 cells:** from the right `H` to the center `L` of `H–L–H`.
- **2 cells:** from that `L` to the left `EO` of `EO–L–EO`.

The **recorded ciphertext start** is **`EO (21,9)`**. Read **two consecutive cells rightward** along row 21:

`EO (21,9) → D (21,10)`

This route connects the previous ciphertext endpoint to the new start through actual rune positions. The choice of these mirrors, the rightward reading direction, and the two-rune length are part of the proposed construction; the key calculations alone do **not** prove this is the only possible path.

## 4. Read and decrypt the ciphertext

Subtract each key rune index from the corresponding ciphertext rune index, **modulo 29**:

`Plaintext index = (ciphertext index − key index) mod 29`

| Cell | Ciphertext | Key | Calculation (mod 29) | Decrypted rune |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| (21,9) | EO = 12 | TH = 2 | 12 − 2 ≡ 10 | **I** |
| (21,10) | D = 23 | H = 8 | 23 − 8 ≡ 15 | **S** |

## Result

**Decrypted rune sequence:** `I · S`

**Proposed reading: IS**

Both modular subtractions reproduce **IS** from the stated cells and selected key alignment.

## 5. A geometric connection at the ciphertext endpoint

The ciphertext finishes at **`D (21,10)`**. This cell is the center of a diagonal mirror:

`E (19,12) → D (21,10) → E (23,8)`

Each outer `E` is **2 diagonal grid steps** from the central `D`.

The key's Möbius signature is **`(0, +1, 0)`**, classified as **CENTER** in the project's geometric interpretation. The selected ciphertext ends at the **center** of `E–D–E`, matching that classification.

This is an **additional geometric correspondence**, not an arithmetic operation needed to decrypt **IS**. It does not independently establish that the key or ciphertext path is unique.
