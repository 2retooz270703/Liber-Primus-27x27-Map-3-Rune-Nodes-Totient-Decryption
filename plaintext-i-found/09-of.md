# 09 — OF

This example shows how the diagonal mirror at the end of **IDEA** supplies the key for **OF**. The new key is then applied to two adjacent ciphertext cells in row 18.

**Notation:** Coordinates are **1-based** and written `(row, column)`. Rune numbers are indices in the **29-rune alphabet**. `φ` is Euler's totient function; `μ` is the Möbius function.

## 1. Find the connected three-rune nodes

The **IDEA** ciphertext ends at **`D (17,18)`**, the center of the diagonal mirror:

`J (15,20) → D (17,18) → J (19,16)`

Each `J` is **2 diagonal grid steps** from the central `D`. The lower `J (19,16)` is also the center of a vertical mirror:

`OE (18,16) → J (19,16) → OE (20,16)`

| Row | Column 15 | Column 16 | Column 17 |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 18 | **X** | **OE** | R |
| **19** | F | **J** | A |
| 20 | I | **OE** | M |

These two mirrors share **`J (19,16)`**: it is an outer rune of `J–D–J` and the center of `OE–J–OE`.

**Key-generating node: `J–D–J`.** The vertical `OE–J–OE` node provides an additional geometric connection and lies beside the selected ciphertext cells.

## 2. Derive and align the decryption key

### Replace the center rune

Keep both outer `J` runes. Replace the center `D` with the rune whose index equals `φ(23)`:

`D = 23 → φ(23) = 22 → OE`

The unrotated key is therefore **`J–OE–J`**.

### Calculate its rotation

For each rune in the derived key, first apply `φ` to its index, then apply `μ` to the result:

| Key rune | Rune index | Totient | Möbius value |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| J | 11 | φ(11) = 10 | μ(10) = +1 |
| OE | 22 | φ(22) = 10 | μ(10) = +1 |
| J | 11 | φ(11) = 10 | μ(10) = +1 |

The **totient signature** is `(10, 10, 10)`, and the **Möbius signature** is `(+1, +1, +1)`.

The Möbius sum determines the number of cyclic left rotations:

`Rotation = (1 + 1 + 1) mod 3 = 0`

No rotation is applied.

**Final repeating key: `J–OE–J`.** Because the ciphertext contains only **two runes**, the decryption uses the first two key positions: **`J–OE`**. The third `J` is not used.

## 3. Locate the ciphertext start

The upper `OE` of the vertical mirror is at **`(18,16)`**. Its immediate left neighbor is **`X (18,15)`**:

`OE (18,16) → left 1 → X (18,15)`

The recorded ciphertext begins at `X (18,15)` and runs **one cell right** to `OE (18,16)`:

`X (18,15) → OE (18,16)`

This places both ciphertext cells next to the shared node `J (19,16)`. The node geometry and the key calculation do **not**, on their own, establish a unique rule requiring this start, direction, or two-rune length; those are the reading choices documented in this example.

## 4. Read and decrypt the ciphertext

Apply the first two positions of the unchanged key `J–OE–J`, subtracting each **key rune index** from its **ciphertext rune index**, modulo 29:

`Plaintext index = (ciphertext index − key index) mod 29`

| Cell | Ciphertext | Key | Calculation (mod 29) | Decrypted rune |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| (18,15) | X = 14 | J = 11 | 14 − 11 ≡ 3 | **O** |
| (18,16) | OE = 22 | OE = 22 | 22 − 22 ≡ 0 | **F** |

## Result

**Decrypted rune sequence:** `O · F`

**Proposed reading: OF**

This reproduces the two-letter plaintext using the stated ciphertext cells and key. The overlap between the `J–D–J` and `OE–J–OE` mirrors is a geometric observation; it does not independently establish that `OF` is the uniquely intended plaintext.
