# 11 — IS

AS I GO, THE WEATHER TURNS COLD. I MAY CRY NOW. THE IDEA OF THE END IS

## 1. END leads to two mirrors with the same center

The previous chapter, [`10-END.md`](./10-END.md), ends at **I(21,23)**. This rune is the right outer of a small horizontal mirror:

```text
I(21,21) — R(21,22) — I(21,23)
```

The center is **R(21,22)**. Transforming its rune value with Euler's totient gives:

```text
R = 4
φ(4) = 2 = TH

I-R-I → I-TH-I
```

The interesting part is that **the same R(21,22) is also the center of a larger mirror** on the very same row:

```text
H(21,17) ── 5 ── R(21,22) ── 5 ── H(21,27)
```

Here the same center transformation produces **H-TH-H**. The two mirrors have different outer runes and different radii, but their transformed rune values share the same totient signature:

```text
I-TH-I:  φ(10), φ(2), φ(10) = (4,1,4)
H-TH-H:  φ(8),  φ(2), φ(8)  = (4,1,4)
```

So the endpoint of `END` connects directly to the **H-TH-H key family** through a shared center and an identical signature.

## 2. H-TH-H generates the key

Now use the larger **H-R-H** mirror as the key source. Its transformed form is **H-TH-H**, and the signature `(4,1,4)` determines the Möbius phase:

```text
Totient signature: (4,1,4)
Möbius signature: (0,+1,0)
Phase:              (0+1+0) mod 3 = 1
```

**Phase 1** rotates `H-TH-H` into `TH-H-H`:

```text
H-TH-H → phase 1 → TH-H-H
```

The next ciphertext contains two runes, so we use the first two positions of this generated key:

```text
Active key: TH-H
```

This key comes from the mirror that shares its center with the ending of `END`.

## 3. Connected mirrors lead to EO-D

The left outer rune of **H-R-H** is **H(21,17)**. It is also the right outer of another horizontal mirror, **H-L-H**:

```text
H(21,5) ── 6 ── L(21,11) ── 6 ── H(21,17)
```

This brings us to **L(21,11)**, which is itself the center of a smaller **EO-L-EO** mirror:

```text
EO(21,9) ── 2 ── L(21,11) ── 2 ── EO(21,13)
```

The mirrors are connected through their shared runes: `H(21,17)` joins the first two structures, and `L(21,11)` joins the last two. Following the left side of **EO-L-EO** brings us to **EO(21,9)**.

Immediately to its right lies **D(21,10)**. Together, these adjacent cells give the ciphertext:

```text
EO(21,9) → D(21,10)

Ciphertext: EO-D
```

We now have the two components needed for decryption: **EO-D** from the connected mirrors and **TH-H** from the shared `R` center.

## 4. Decrypting EO-D

Subtract the active key from the ciphertext, modulo 29:

| Ciphertext | Key | Calculation | Plaintext |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EO = 12` | `TH = 2` | `12 − 2 = 10` | **I** |
| `D = 23` | `H = 8` | `23 − 8 = 15` | **S** |

```text
Ciphertext: EO - D
Key:        TH - H
Plaintext:  I  - S
```

The result is **IS**. The word ends at **D(21,10)**, a position that also creates an important connection to the next stage.

## 5. The endpoint repeats the 010 → CENTER pattern

The key-generating structure **H-TH-H** has Möbius signature **`(0,+1,0)`**. In the project's route model, this pattern is associated with a **CENTER** continuation.

The final rune of `IS`, **D(21,10)**, is exactly the center of a diagonal mirror:

```text
E(19,12) ── 2 ── D(21,10) ── 2 ── E(23,8)
```

This matches the same pattern previously encountered at two other endpoints:

| Word | Final cell | Mirror centered there |
|---|---|---|
| `COLD` | `A(11,7)` | `EA-A-EA` |
| `IDEA` | `D(17,18)` | `J-D-J` |
| `IS` | `D(21,10)` | `E-D-E` |

In all three cases, the **`(0,+1,0)` signature** is followed by a plaintext endpoint located at the **center of another mirror**. For `IS`, that mirror is `E-D-E`.

## 6. The final D reconnects with the key for DEATH

The `IS` key has **phase 1**. At the endpoint **D(21,10)**, the coordinate selector gives:

```text
φ(21) = 12 → μ(12) = 0
φ(10) =  4 → μ(4)  = 0

V₁(21,10) = (0,0)
```

Because neither Möbius component supplies a direction, the nearby **E-D-E** mirror provides the next geometric connection. Its outer runes are two rows and two columns from the center. Using the same distance **perpendicular to this diagonal** leads two rows down and two columns right:

```text
D(21,10) ── (+2,+2) ──→ B(23,12)
```

This **B(23,12)** is the center of a familiar mirror:

```text
J(19,16) ── 4 ── B(23,12) ── 4 ── J(27,8)
```

The **J-B-J** structure previously generated the key for `END`. Reaching its center again connects the ending of `IS` to the next chapter, [`12-DEATH.md`](./12-DEATH.md), where that same mirror regenerates **J-J-T** and decrypts **DEATH**.
